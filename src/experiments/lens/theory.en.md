## Thin lenses

A lens is **thin** when its thickness is small compared with its focal length. Then every ray can be treated as bending once, in the plane of the lens.

- A **converging** lens (thicker in the middle) gathers rays parallel to the axis at the focus $F$ behind it.
- A **diverging** lens (thinner in the middle) spreads such rays as if they came from the focus in front of it.

The **optical power** is $D = \dfrac{1}{F}$, in dioptres (1 dpt = 1 m⁻¹). For diverging lenses $F$ and $D$ are negative.

## Three characteristic rays

From the top of the object it is enough to draw two of these rays:

1. A ray **parallel to the axis** passes, after the lens, through the focus behind it (converging) or seems to come from the focus in front of it (diverging).
2. A ray **through the optical centre** does not change direction.
3. A ray **through the focus** in front of a converging lens (or aimed at the far focus of a diverging lens) leaves the lens parallel to the axis.

Where the refracted rays meet, a **real** image forms; it can be caught on a screen. If they diverge, their backward extensions (dashed) meet at a **virtual** image, which can only be seen through the lens.

## The thin lens formula

With $d$ the distance from the object to the lens and $f$ from the lens to the image:

$$\frac{1}{F} = \frac{1}{d} + \frac{1}{f}, \qquad \Gamma = \frac{H}{h} = \frac{|f|}{d}.$$

Sign rules used here: $F > 0$ for a converging and $F < 0$ for a diverging lens; $f > 0$ for a real image and $f < 0$ for a virtual one.

## Images of a converging lens

| Object position | Image |
|---|---|
| $d > 2F$ | real, inverted, reduced, between $F$ and $2F$ |
| $d = 2F$ | real, inverted, same size, at $2F$ |
| $F < d < 2F$ | real, inverted, enlarged, beyond $2F$ |
| $d = F$ | no image: the rays leave parallel |
| $d < F$ | virtual, upright, enlarged (a magnifying glass) |

A diverging lens always gives a virtual, upright, reduced image.

## Sharpness on a screen

A lens of aperture $A$ focuses each point of the object into a point at distance $f$. A screen at another distance $s$ shows a blur spot of diameter

$$c = A\,\frac{|s - f|}{f}.$$

The image is sharp only when the screen is at the image. In the simulation $A = 14$ cm.

## How the lab works

Rewrite the formula as $\dfrac{1}{f} = -\dfrac{1}{d} + \dfrac{1}{F}$. Plot $1/f$ against $1/d$ (both in dioptres), fit a straight line, and the intercept gives $D = 1/F$, so $F = 1/b$. The slope should be close to $-1$.

## Assumptions of the model

- The lens is thin and free of aberrations; all rays follow the thin lens formula.
- The drawing may stretch the vertical scale so that small objects stay visible; the label on the scene shows the factor. Positions along the axis are to scale.
