## Where the buoyant force comes from

Pressure in a liquid grows with depth: $p = p_0 + \rho g h$. A submerged body feels a larger pressure on its bottom face than on its top face. For a cube of side $a$ with area $S = a^2$:

$$F_A = (p_{bottom} - p_{top})\,S = \rho_l g a \cdot a^2 = \rho_l g V.$$

## Archimedes' principle

A body immersed in a liquid is pushed up with a force equal to the weight of the liquid it displaces:

$$F_A = \rho_l\, g\, V_{sub},$$

where $\rho_l$ is the density of the liquid and $V_{sub}$ the submerged volume. The buoyant force does not depend on the material of the body or on the depth (once the body is completely under the surface).

## Weighing in a liquid

A spring scale holding the body shows

$$F = mg - F_A = \rho_b g V - \rho_l g V_{sub}.$$

A measuring cylinder shows how much the liquid level rises: the increase is exactly the submerged volume $V_{sub}$.

## Sinking and floating

Fully submerged, the body is pushed up with $\rho_l g V$ and pulled down with $\rho_b g V$:

- $\rho_b > \rho_l$: the body sinks;
- $\rho_b < \rho_l$: the body floats up and stays at the surface with the fraction $\dfrac{V_{sub}}{V} = \dfrac{\rho_b}{\rho_l}$ submerged;
- $\rho_b = \rho_l$: the body hovers at any depth.

That is why ice floats in water with about 90% under the surface, and iron floats in mercury.

## How the lab works

The scale reading depends linearly on the displaced volume:

$$F = P - \rho_l g\,V_{sub}.$$

Plot $F$ against $V_{sub}$ and fit a straight line $F = kV + b$. Then $\rho_l = -k/g$ (with $V$ converted to m³) and $b = P = \rho_b g V$ is the weight in air. With the body fully submerged, the displaced volume equals the body volume, so $\rho_b = \dfrac{b}{gV}$.

## Assumptions of the model

- The body is a cube; the liquid is incompressible and at rest.
- Surface tension and the volume of the string are ignored.
- The cylinder has an inner cross-section of 50 cm² and contains 400 cm³ of liquid; its scale has a 5 cm³ division.
